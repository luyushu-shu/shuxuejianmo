\centerline{\zihao{-3}\bfseries ABSTRACT}

\linespread{1.3}\zihao{-4}\bigskip

This report studies a high-precision parameter determination problem for nonlinear ODE systems. Based on the provided materials, especially \texttt{PredatorPreyProblem.tex} and associated derivative codes, we reconstruct a complete workflow from model formulation to robust identification.

The system is represented by state and observation equations with measurement noise, and parameter estimation is formulated as nonlinear least squares. Gauss--Newton and damped Newton methods are developed as the core optimization engines. In simulation, high-order Taylor expansion with adaptive step-size control is used to improve trajectory accuracy and parameter sensitivity.

To address practical difficulties such as initialization sensitivity, noise amplification, and local minima, we further introduce multi-start initialization, regularization constraints, and Monte Carlo perturbation tests. Numerical results indicate that Gauss--Newton converges efficiently in low-to-medium noise settings, while damping strategies significantly improve robustness under higher noise and poor initial guesses.

\bigskip
\noindent{\bfseries Key words:}
parameter determination; ordinary differential equations; Gauss--Newton; damped Newton; robustness analysis
